Want a PM salary? Tell me what you actually manage.

If I could ask a PM candidate only one question, it would be this:

"Walk me through your last project. What were you responsible for, and what did you actually do in each phase of the SDLC?"

Give them 5–10 minutes.

I'm listening for decisions, responsibilities, and results. 
A polished list of PM verbs won't take long to fall apart.

These are the nine areas I want to explore.

→ Initiation. Why did the project exist? Who needed it? What made it worth doing?
→ Planning. Scope, estimates, resources, risks, schedule, budget.
→ Analysis. How did you get people to agree on what they actually needed?
→ Design. How did you coordinate decisions and the necessary approvals?
→ Development. What did you do when reality started eating the plan?
→ Testing. How did you organize quality checks and agree on acceptance?
→ Deployment. Who had to be ready before the release could happen?
→ Closure. Who accepted the result? What did you hand over?
→ Maintenance. What was your role in support, stability, and changes?

My rough read of the answer:

① Beginner. Covers 2–4 areas. Broad phrases. Needs constant prompting to explain their own work.

② Intermediate. Covers 3–5 areas. Mostly concrete, with gaps worth digging into.

③ Professional. Covers 6–9 areas. Explains decisions and responsibilities clearly. Has examples ready when asked.

Different phase names? Fine.
Different lifecycle? Explain how it worked and where your responsibility started and ended.

And if someone names all nine but can't explain a single decision, we have a vocabulary test result.

"I coordinated development" gives me very little.
What did you decide? With whom? What changed because of it?

That's where the interview gets interesting.

Try answering the question about your own last project.
Set a timer. You have ten minutes.

If you get stuck somewhere between "managed" and "controlled", write to me.
We can work through your experience and prepare your answer in a consultation.
