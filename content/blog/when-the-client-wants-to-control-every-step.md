## The questions people bring to consultations

Several times people have come to me for a consultation with questions like these:

- Should the client have direct contact with the team?
- Should they be allowed at the dailies? What about the retrospective?

The short answer: first find out why the client needs it.

## What direct client-team contact costs you

The main risks of too much communication between the client and the team:

```diagram
type: groups
title: Three groups of risks when the client talks to the team too much
# Productivity and time
- Extra calls
- Context switching
- Scope creep
# Psychological and team risks
- Blurred and lost accountability
- Loss of trust
- Demotivation
# Financial and management risks
- Higher project cost
- Falling margins
- Slow decisions
```

## Where the urge to control every step comes from

Where does this urge even come from?

- They aren't getting [the information they need](/blog/why-one-way-reporting-kills-projects/)?
- The team has already broken agreements?
- They don't know how the company's processes work?
- Or is it simply a habit of running everything personally?

```visual
type: iceberg
title: What hides behind the urge to control every step
top: "You won't figure it out without me!"
topnote: what the team hears
- Missing information | the client isn't getting what they need | eye
- Broken agreements | the team has broken them before | warning
- Unfamiliar processes | the client doesn't know how the company works | gear
- Habit | running everything personally | control
```

Get to the bottom of the cause, and it's easy to find a solution that fixes the problem without breaking your processes.

## What to do: cause, roles, meetings, communication format

PMI materials on leadership say that trust and team autonomy matter.
And Controlling Interest suggests finding out the reasons behind excessive interference.
Agree on roles, meeting attendance and [the communication format](/blog/the-real-cost-of-a-quick-two-minute-call/).

```visual
type: flow
title: How to bring the client back inside agreed boundaries
end: effect
- Find the cause | where the urge to interfere comes from | search
- Agree on roles | who gives tasks to whom | team
- Agree on meeting attendance | dailies, retrospective | calendar
- Agree on the communication format | how and where we talk | chat
- Problem solved | without breaking your processes | check
```

## Red-flag phrases in client communication

The worst red flags you can spot when talking to a client:

```diagram
type: cards
mark: cross
title: Phrases that should put you on alert
- "I pay you money." | So I can give tasks to whoever I want, whenever I want.
- "If you don't like the way I communicate," | that's your personal problem.
- "You signed the contract," | so deliver on it and stop complaining to me.
```
