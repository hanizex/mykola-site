The "boring" candidate is the one it's easier to make an offer to than to explain why you've decided to move forward with other candidates ©

## How to respond to a recruiter's rejection

Here are my 8 strategies for responding to rejections from recruiters.
Each one works in its own situation.

## The reason, a call and other roles

**1. Ask them to explain the reason for the rejection in more detail.**
Works great when they didn't even look at your CV and replied with a standard, impersonal brush-off.

**2. Ask for a 15-minute call with the recruiter.**
Helps when you need to present yourself better than your CV manages to.

**3. Ask about other roles currently open at the company.**
Ask straight away for the contacts of the recruiter or the hiring manager, so you skip the cold application and the ATS check.

## A pilot, a referral and value-first

**4. Offer a paid "pilot": 2-4 weeks of real work instead of interviews.**
Works when they've already picked another candidate.
It won't get you a full hire, but it gives you a chance to show what you can do and maybe land a contract too.

**5. Ask for a referral into their network.**
Works well after you've had interviews and they tell you they're "impressed by your skills".
Let them recommend you to their peers through their own channels.

**6. "Value-first": get ahead of the game.**
Prepare and send something genuinely useful to show your intentions and skills.
Works great when you know the company's pains and needs.

## Agree on SMART terms instead of "We'll get back to you"

**7. Agree on SMART terms or set OKRs.**
Agree on specific actions within a specific time frame with a measurable result.
So instead of "We'll get back to you" you get "Within 3 days I'll check openings with other recruiters and send you the details with contacts".

## A joke as the last move

**8. A joke as the last move.**
Works great when you've tried every other way, nothing has worked, you have nothing to lose, and you just want to get at least a little satisfaction out of all the effort you've put in!
