Does a PM need a technical background?

Short answer: NO.

## My technical background

→ I wrote Assembler code for 20+ different processor types and did reverse engineering.
→ I programmed in Basic, Pascal, C, C++, Python, PHP and JavaScript.
→ I built websites, parsers, generators, analysers and other fun stuff.
→ I set up computer networks and configured Windows and Linux servers for all kinds of needs.

But I can't say any of it helped my career as a project manager.

It may even have worked against me. I had to fight my own biases and beliefs, and learn to stay out of other people's business.

## Employers don't know what they want either

Among the reasons I was refused a job offer, you'll find both of these:

→ "Not enough technical background."
→ "Too technical" (because I would tell developers how to do their job).

```diagram
type: compare
title: Two opposite reasons for a rejection
left: Rejection 1
right: Rejection 2
- "Not enough technical background" | "Too technical"
- First instinct: sign up for a coding course | The explanation: I would tell developers how to do their job
```

So if you were turned down because of your background, don't rush to sign up for a coding course. The next company may reject you for the exact opposite.

## What you actually need

Management in IT requires an understanding of the basics and principles of software development. So you can talk properly with every stakeholder: developers, testers, designers, the client.

But you don't have to learn programming languages.

It's enough to listen carefully to your colleagues and ask clarifying questions.

## What to learn instead of code

These days I'd rather recommend exploring what generative AI can do and levelling up your English.
