"Mykola, what do we even pay you this money for? What do you do all day?"

## Why a manager's work is invisible

Great questions. And I understand the people who ask them.
From the outside, my work can be easy to miss.

There are no fires around me.
I don't run at the team shouting and making threats.
I don't churn out hundreds of documents and force people to read them.

I spend a lot of time observing, studying, thinking, analysing and planning. Then I do the thing that will have the expected positive effect for months or years.

```visual
type: flow
title: What my work looks like
end: effect
- Observe and analyse | study, think, plan | eye
- One simple action | the thing that brings the result | idea
- A chain reaction | up, down and across the company | cycle
- Effect for months or years | the expected positive effect | up
- The system holds itself up | no overseer needed any more | check
```

It doesn't look like a revolution. Nobody loses the feeling of solid ground under their feet after it.

## Brute force works too. But why bother?

You can work with brute force.
You can [shout, demand](/blog/fear-is-not-respect/) and micromanage.
You can bury everyone in rules and instructions.
You can set unrealistic goals and expectations.

But why? It's expensive, stressful and dangerous.
It often leads to burnout and lost resources.

You can use that approach in spots, very carefully and for a limited time.

## "But it just works on its own"

And when I finish my work, it turns out I'm no longer needed there. The architecture is built so that the system supports itself, and an overseer isn't really needed any more.

For me that's the sign I did my job well and did it right.

... but then how do you explain what you should pay me for?

It just works on its own.
And I "didn't do anything"...

## Measure first, then set goals

That question taught me to work really well with expectations, goals and metrics.

```diagram
type: steps
title: Before I start the work
- Measure | What exists and how it works
- Collect data | Statistics are both insurance and working material
- Set goals | OKRs, SLOs
- Get to work | Now there is a direction and there are limits
```

The data you have shows more than the direction you need to move in.
It also sets the limits: what you can do and what is "not in the budget".

## A goal sets the budget too

For example, if the goal is "cut Cycle Time by 20%", the team delivers more in the same time and the business gets results sooner. That has a price in money. Let's say XXX$ a year.

And if my approach costs the company YYY$ over the same year, and YYY > XXX, those changes aren't worth making.

## What I'm hired for and what the business gets

This is the most valuable part of my work, when I do what I'm [hired](/blog/dont-hire-me/) to do:

```diagram
type: groups
title: A manager's work and the business's money
# What I'm hired to do
- Bring order to chaos
- Set up, fix and optimise processes
- Make planning and budget management more transparent
- Work through risks, make the system more stable
- Build a system for upskilling and training the team
- Implement Agile and Lean best practices
# What the business gets in the end
- Saved money here
- Didn't lose it there
- Didn't have to spend it again here
- And here started doing and earning more
```

In the end, all of this gives the business MONEY.
