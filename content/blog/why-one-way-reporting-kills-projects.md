"I'm the manager, and I don't have to tell the team what I'm working on or explain my decisions."

If you say this sentence out loud, congratulations: that's control, not management. One-way reporting creates a theatre of "green statuses" while the money goes into the bin.

A team must see what is happening in the system it works in.

## Why one-way reporting destroys a project

- **Orders without explanations** remove the team's ability to work on efficiency or suggest improvements.
- **Decisions that look arbitrary**, because nobody sees the logic or the price of the change, kill engagement and initiative.
- **Priorities that change without explanation.** What was important yesterday is not today, and nobody says why. Trust in priorities disappears, the mission stops mattering, and motivation goes to zero.
- **Reporting becomes a way to avoid punishment.** When the team doesn't understand what is truly valuable, reports are written to stay safe. That blinds the management and makes it impossible to react to real threats to the business.

When a manager does not give context, priorities and real needs, people fill the gaps with guesses. That costs extra meetings, rework and lost speed, where one honest conversation would have been enough.

## My protocol for regular, open communication

1. **State of priorities.** What is first right now, and what is not worth spending attention on.
2. **Reasons and consequences of decisions.** Which factors influenced it, what danger we are avoiding, which problem we are fighting.
3. **Constraints.** Budget, time, conditions, dependencies and blockers. Every detail matters: clear boundaries let people find solutions that satisfy the requirements and expectations.
4. **Results of hypothesis checks.** What worked, what didn't, what we plan to change, which new questions appeared and which blind spots are still unexplored.
5. **My own commitments and action plan.** What I, as the manager, will do to remove blockers and resolve conflicts between parties, with a clear timeline.

The format is secondary. What matters is that everyone sees one picture: *our goals and values are the same*, and *I carry responsibility for the success of the project together with you*.

Two-way reporting rebuilds a reliable management dynamic and keeps the whole system in one field of view for everyone.

What did your manager (or you, if you manage) not say openly to the team recently, although saying it would have improved the team's work that same day?
