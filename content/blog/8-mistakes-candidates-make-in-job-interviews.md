You can lose an offer before you answer a single question.

I'm often asked how to prepare for job interviews.
Here are eight mistakes I see most often, and what to do instead.

## 1. Arriving late without communicating

If you leave people waiting and guessing, you've already demonstrated how you handle an agreement.

Confirm the time zone, set reminders and leave a buffer.
If something goes wrong, notify the interviewer as soon as you can and suggest a solution.

## 2. Skipping the technical check before an online interview

An online interview shouldn't become a tutorial on finding your microphone.

Test the actual platform, sound and camera beforehand.
Choose a quiet setting and prepare a backup connection.
If something fails, explain it and agree on how to continue.

## 3. Knowing nothing about the company or role

"I just need a job" may be honest. It gives the company very little reason to choose you.

Research the business and the role.
Prepare questions about project goals, expectations and the problems you're being hired to solve.

## 4. Answering a different question

The interviewer asks about a decision.
You deliver a fifteen-minute history of your department.

Give the answer first. Then add a relevant example: your responsibility, your action, the result.
If the question is unclear, ask for clarification.

## 5. Bluffing or hiding outside assistance

A polished definition won't save you when the next question is "Why did you do that?"

Explain what you know in your own words.
If you don't know, say so.

Use AI to practise before the interview.
Agree on tool use during it.
Passing generated answers off as your own experience is still bluffing.

## 6. Turning a previous job into a character assassination

You can describe a badly managed project without presenting a list of idiots who worked there.

Explain what happened, how it affected the work and what you did.
Keep criticism specific.
Save jokes at other people's expense for another occasion.

## 7. Apologising until the original mistake becomes a footnote

A wrong answer needs a correction.
It rarely needs your entire autobiography.

"I gave the wrong figure. The correct number is X."

Acknowledge it, correct it and move on.
Repeated apologies consume time you could spend explaining your experience.

## 8. Letting interview nerves derail your answers

Your hands can shake while your answer still makes sense.

Record a mock interview.
Check your pace and whether your answers are easy to follow.
Practise pausing, asking for a moment and returning to the question.

## How to prepare for your next job interview

Run one practice interview before the real one.
Check the call setup, research the role and prepare examples that show your contribution.
Use these eight mistakes as a review checklist.
