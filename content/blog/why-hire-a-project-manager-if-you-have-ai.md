Why hire project managers at all?
What good are they if you already have AI?

Paying a manager's salary for meeting notes and dragging tickets around Jira is an expensive pleasure.

## What you can already hand over to AI

Reports, meeting notes and draft plans can already be automated.

If that's all your PM does, you don't have a project manager. You have an expensive secretary.

```diagram
type: compare
title: Where AI's job ends
left: What AI already does
right: What a PM is paid for
- Meeting notes | Decisions that cost money and trust
- Reports | Owning those decisions
- Draft plans | Saying "no" to a client without losing the contract
```

## What companies still pay a project manager for

```visual
type: hub
title: Seven things AI won't do for your PM
center: Project manager
- Business problem | target
- Priorities, budget, deadlines | money
- Authority | shield
- Why work stalls | search
- Uncomfortable conversations | chat
- Decisions on change and risk | balance
- AI in the workflow | robot
```

→ **Find out which business problem we are actually solving.**
Agree on success criteria. And say in time that the proposed project won't solve that problem.

→ **Agree priorities, budget and deadlines with stakeholders.**
Especially when everyone wants their own thing, and someone else is supposed to pay for all of it.

→ **Align responsibilities and authority.**
So people understand what is expected of them and are actually able to deliver it.

→ **Find the reason the work is stalling.**
Unclear requirements, conflicts, missing skills or overload each need a different fix. One more status meeting doesn't always help.

→ **Have the uncomfortable conversations.**
Tell the client "no". Explain to management the consequences of their decision. Talk to an employee about broken agreements.

→ **Get decisions made on changes and risks.**
Someone has to approve extra costs, a moved deadline or dropping part of the scope. While the team can still deliver the new plan.

→ **Bring AI into the workflow.**
Decide what agents do on their own, where a human has to sign off, and who owns the result.

## The bottom line

When a decision costs the company money and trust, you can't shift the responsibility for it onto an AI tool.

AI will write your meeting notes. But telling the client "no" and keeping the contract is still a job for a human.
