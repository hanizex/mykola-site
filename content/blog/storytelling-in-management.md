Storytelling in management is a tool for aligning intentions.

## The 48-hour test: a story has to change behaviour

```diagram
type: quote
text: If your story doesn't change how people behave within 48 hours, it isn't leadership. It's cheap entertainment at a corporate party.
```

The most expensive mistake is confusing a "beautiful presentation" with a leadership narrative.

## What actually has value

A beautiful story and polished slides are not the end value.
The value is in the model of reality you create, one where it's clear:

```visual
type: hub
title: The model of reality a story has to create
center: Model of reality
- What should happen | flag
- Who owns it | user
- What the risk is | warning
- What counts as failure | x
- Top priority | target
```

Without that, your "mission" becomes set decoration and your team becomes the audience.

## Why storytelling either works for the system or hurts it

Five scenarios where the story does damage:

```diagram
type: compare
title: When storytelling hurts the system
left: What you do
right: What you get
- You talk about "the future" but don't name the cost of change today | People hear a fairy tale, not a plan
- You sell heroism instead of a framework | Heroism scales badly and always ends in burnout
- You smooth over a conflict of priorities with "one visionary storyline" | That's not unity, it's a postponed decision that will still blow up in production
- You say "we're a family" when you should say "we're a results machine with clear rules" | A family doesn't fire for incompetence. A system does, if a node is breaking the pace
- You repeat the story but don't tie it to metrics and checkpoints | It's PR for the boss, not navigation for the team
```

## My framework for a leadership narrative

- **One storyline per quarter:** what exactly we start building and what we stop doing for good. Without the second part, the narrative lies.
- **Every chapter ends with an artefact:** a decision, an owner, a deadline, a definition of done. A story without an artefact doesn't manage, it hypnotises.
- **The logic of change is public:** "why this way" and "why not another way". When people don't understand the constraints, they [invent enemies](/blog/why-one-way-reporting-kills-projects/).
- **A hard test:** if your words disappeared tomorrow, would the process still move? If not, you're not the architect, you're just the voice-over.

## A narrative is either a system or nonsense

Either your narrative brings intention, [responsibility](/blog/project-manager-seniority-is-responsibility/) and effectiveness together into one system, or you're just voicing nonsense at great expense and wondering why "nobody got on board".
