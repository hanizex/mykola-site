In remote teams, group video calls are one of the main ways people communicate. And they often turn into a show of filters, backgrounds and other special effects.

## The group video call show

```diagram
type: cards
mark: dot
title: Who you meet on a typical group call
- No camera | Someone systematically refuses to turn it on
- Behind an avatar | Someone hides behind a fried egg or a cat
- No sound | The mic is set up so you can't hear a thing
- With a soundtrack | You hear clothes rustling, coffee slurping and a sandwich being chewed
```

## Some find it fun, others find it annoying

For some people it's totally fine: a bit of a laugh, fun, hilarious.
Others find it annoying, and for some it feels like distrust and disrespect.

It's important to be able to ask politely for respect and for your boundaries to be kept.

## How a team gets used to each other

```diagram
type: steps
title: From friction to acceptance
- Friction | Some find it fun, others find it annoying
- Asking | We politely talk about respect and boundaries
- Adapting | Over time the friction disappears
- Acceptance | Everyone can be themselves without tension or stress
```

What you end up with is a great feeling of freedom and acceptance: everyone can be themselves without creating tension or adding stress on video calls.

## An internal call and a client call are different things

Of course, a team's internal calls are very different from the ones with clients and other stakeholders.
There, corporate and business culture demands formality, strictness, politeness and order.

```diagram
type: compare
title: Our calls and their calls
left: Internal team call
right: Call with clients and stakeholders
- You can be yourself | Formality
- Freedom | Strictness
- Acceptance | Politeness and order
```
