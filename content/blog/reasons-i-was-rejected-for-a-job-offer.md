I was turned down for a job because I wasn't competent enough.
And because I was overqualified. In other words, too smart.

Here are the reasons I have been rejected for a job offer.
It's probably not the full list, but the main ones are here.

## 36 rejection reasons, grouped by theme

```diagram
type: groups
title: 36 reasons I was rejected for a job offer
caption: The number next to each theme is how many reasons it holds
# Skills and experience
- Not competent enough
- Overqualified
- Not enough technical background
- Too technical
- No PMP® certificate
- Worked for more than 3 companies, some for only 3-6 months
# Money and terms
- Salary expectations too high
- Salary expectations too low
- Refused to work overtime and on weekends
- Refused 50% pay during the probation period
- No MacBook and iPhone of my own for work
# Who and where I am
- Strong Ukrainian accent in English
- Too old (40+)
- 5 kids
- Ukrainian (and the company has Russians on the team)
- Living abroad
- Not ready to return to Ukraine to work on-site on a miltech project
- No local work visa and no local bank account
# At the interview
- No "spark" in my eyes
- Didn't ask clarifying questions or questions about the company
- Asked too many clarifying questions
- Talked too much about myself
- Talked too much about the value for the company
- Rescheduled the interview
- Wore a T-shirt, not a suit
- Did the test task with the help of AI
- Didn't send a follow-up myself after the technical interview
# Outside work
- LinkedIn activity
- My pet projects
- My volunteering
- Posting memes
- Work experience in my CV didn't match LinkedIn
# How I look for a job
- Added the OpenToWork frame
- Didn't add the OpenToWork frame
- Talking to other companies in parallel
- Not talking to other companies
```

## Rejected for one thing. And for the opposite

Some of the reasons come in mirror pairs.
One company turned me down for one thing. Another turned me down for the exact opposite.

```diagram
type: compare
title: A rejection reason and its opposite
left: Rejected because...
right: ...and rejected because
- Not competent enough | Overqualified (too smart)
- Not enough technical background | Too technical (I'll tell developers how to do their job)
- Salary expectations too high | Salary expectations too low (I'll leave for whoever offers more)
- Didn't ask clarifying questions (not interested) | Asked too many clarifying questions (suspicious)
- Talked too much about myself (should have talked about value for the company) | Talked too much about value for the company (should have talked about myself)
- Added the OpenToWork frame (a sign of desperation) | Didn't add the OpenToWork frame (so not open to offers)
- Talking to other companies in parallel (so I don't really want this one) | Not talking to other companies (so I'm not pursuing my goals hard enough)
```

Seven pairs. Fourteen of the thirty-six reasons.

## The best part is in the brackets

The reasons are only half the story. The other half is the explanations that came with them.

→ Too old: 40+, won't fit in with the team, will work all the time, and anyway I'm just a stupid old man.
→ 5 kids: a risk that I won't be able to work remotely.
→ LinkedIn activity: takes too much time, a risk that I'll leak confidential data, a risk that someone will poach me.
→ My pet projects: a risk that they'll start making serious money and I'll quit.
→ My volunteering: a risk that I'll spend my working hours on it.
→ Didn't send a follow-up after the technical interview: showed no initiative and no interest.
→ Rescheduled the interview: can't manage my time, can't adjust to everyone.
→ Living abroad: not a patriot.
→ Worked for more than 3 companies, some for 3-6 months: unreliable.
→ Posting memes: not serious.

## What this means for a candidate

For every rejection reason, there is an opposite one.
You cannot fit everyone's expectations.
So don't treat every rejection as a verdict.
