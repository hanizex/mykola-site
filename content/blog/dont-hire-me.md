DON'T HIRE ME!

Seriously.

Not every company needs a manager.

## Who some companies are really looking for

Some need a secretary, a dispatcher, a corporate dummy or a human pinger: "Any updates?"

I'm not a fit for any of those roles.

```diagram
type: compare
title: Who they look for instead of a manager
left: What some companies need
right: What a manager does
- Secretary | Makes it clear who does what, when and why
- Dispatcher | A partner, not a messenger between leadership and the team
- Corporate dummy | Has the authority and drives agreed changes
- Human pinger: "Any updates?" | Talks openly about problems, constraints and the real state of projects
```

## Don't hire me if you...

```diagram
type: cards
mark: cross
title: 10 reasons not to hire me
- Don't understand what a manager is responsible for
- Want to hand me routine tasks that should have been automated long ago
- Are looking for someone to blame for any failure
- Hand out responsibility but don't delegate authority
- Expect results but hide the information needed to make decisions
- See a manager as an unavoidable administrative evil and a waste of money
- Judge a manager by the number of meetings, messages and hours online
- Expect a manager to have no personal life and no need for rest
- Want change but aren't ready to change the way you're used to working
- Have no growth strategy or scaling plan and no intention of writing one
```

A manager without goals and authority is an expensive calendar with a reminder function.

I've [covered separately](/blog/why-hire-a-project-manager-if-you-have-ai/) which routine tasks you can already hand over to AI.

## Who should hire me

Hire me if your company:

- wants a clear picture of who does what, when and why
- is ready to talk openly about problems, constraints and the [real state of projects](/blog/why-one-way-reporting-kills-projects/)
- listens to proposals for optimising processes and costs
- gives the authority needed and backs the rollout of agreed changes
- knows how to count money and doesn't waste the team's or the manager's time
- is ready to make decisions instead of looking for excuses why not now
- sees a manager as a partner, not a messenger between leadership and the team
- understands how professional management affects delivery speed, costs, revenue and profit
- has the ambition to grow and is ready to build a manageable system for it

Enough of these mathematically justified excuses in reports!

I work for results:

- The business gets stronger.
- Revenue and profitability go up.
- Goals are reached faster and more easily.
