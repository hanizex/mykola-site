A Personal Development Plan is a legal tool for manipulating people so that they become the way I like them.
© Mykola K.

## What a Personal Development Plan (PDP) is

The way I see it (in the spirit of PMI's competency development framework, PMCDF), the plan has two foundations and four components:

```diagram
type: groups
title: What a PDP is made of
# It is based on
- An assessment of the current level
- The desired result
# It defines
- Actions
- Timelines
- Resources
- Progress criteria
```

## How to build a PDP in practice

In practice:

```diagram
type: cards
mark: check
title: Three things a PDP can't work without
- Who builds it | The team lead, HR, the PM and the employee can all be involved in building the PDP.
- What to agree on | Goals, timelines and progress criteria have to be agreed.
- What to write down | What support the company provides.
```

## Some people don't give a shit about a PDP!

They cope with their work and are happy with their current role.
Their main motivation is for everything to stay the same and not change.

If a person has no wish to change anything, don't waste time on a formal plan.
*Even if the manager's handbook says everyone must have a PDP.*

## A PDP doesn't guarantee a promotion or a raise

A PDP and continuous learning are not, on their own, a guarantee of a [grade promotion](/blog/project-manager-seniority-is-responsibility/) or a salary review.
Most of the time they are seen as part of the normal workflow, part of your direct duties.
So these changes have to be initiated separately.

For example:

– Mykola, I want a salary review!

– ~~(What, again?)~~ I'm listening. What has changed?

– I've done the training from my PDP, and now with my AI sidekick I do three times more, and better, than before.

– Fair enough, that's a solid argument. Here's the deal: you run a workshop for your colleagues and share your hacks, so they can work three times faster and better too, and I write the request for your raise.

(win-win)

## Simple in theory, unclear in practice

In words and in theory it all looks very simple, but in practice it is often unclear:

- What should the plan include for non-technical roles?
- How does a PDP overlap with OKRs, and how is it different?
- Should the learning happen during working hours or outside them?
- And what do you do with people who [sabotage their own PDP and get in everyone else's way](/blog/developer-who-does-not-want-to-work/)?
