"How do you get through to a developer who doesn't want to work?"

## The request: no proof, only a feeling

Someone came to me for a consultation with exactly this request:

> The manager feels the developer works 3-4 hours a day.
> The team is remote, with no time tracker.
> So there is no direct proof, only a personal feeling
> and a comparison with how productive the rest of the team is.

## First: what problem are we actually solving?

The first thing I ask is which problem we are actually solving.

```diagram
type: cards
mark: arrow
title: What problem are we actually solving?
- Is he missing deadlines?
- Are there quality problems?
- Is he blocking the team?
- Is he unavailable at the agreed times?
```

## A manager's goal is results, not hours

```diagram
type: quote
text: A manager's goal is the project result: on time, within budget, at the expected quality.
by: Mykola Kalakutskyi
```

Driving everyone into the fucking ground with work is not part of that job.

If the project is on track and agreements are kept, why demand more hours?
Better to have him [teach the others](/blog/personal-development-plan-as-manipulation/) to get their tasks done faster too, so everyone can rest more.

## If there really is a problem: a conversation in three steps

If there really is a problem, I suggest the Managing underperformance guidance from the Fair Work Ombudsman.

In a one-on-one conversation you need to do three things:

```diagram
type: steps
title: The one-on-one about performance
- Name the broken agreement | "Your tasks are regularly delayed. That blocks other people and affects the whole project."
- Find out the cause | Missing skills? Unclear tasks? Too much workload? Personal circumstances?
- Agree on a solution | Help, mentoring, a change in workload or working conditions.
```

## When to prepare a replacement

If the person deceives the team or sabotages the work, or keeps breaking agreements after getting support, we prepare a replacement and start ending the cooperation.

In consultations and mentoring I help you understand what is really going on and find the cause.
After that, choosing how to solve it is much easier.
