A manager's level, their "grade" or "seniority", is first of all not the number of years on the job. It is the level of personal responsibility.

## Three stages of responsibility

```diagram
type: ladder
title: How a manager's responsibility grows
- Start | I own the tasks handed down to me. Everything else is not mine
- Experience | I take on more because it's better for the project. But the line is still blurry
- Realisation | Everything that happens on the project is ultimately my responsibility
```

## First: only what's handed down

When we're just starting out, we don't know much.
We mostly learn on the tasks that are handed down to us directly.

Everything else is (quite legitimately) not our responsibility.

## Then: taking on more, because it's better for the project

Over time we settle in, gain experience, and it becomes clear that some things have to become our responsibility too.
Not because someone said so, but because it's better for the project.

But we don't yet have enough experience or authority to say for sure: is this my responsibility already, or not yet?

## And suddenly: everything on the project is ours

And then, suddenly, a moment of pure clarity arrives.

```diagram
type: quote
text: Everything that happens on the project, regardless of what was agreed, one way or another, is ultimately our responsibility.
```

There's no way back after that.
You can't "unsee" it.
It becomes part of who you are.

## Where it shows

It shows very clearly in negotiations.
It's very easy to spot in a crisis.

On the inside, it doesn't feel like a big change.
It's mostly visible from the outside.
