What can a team do when there's nothing to do yet?

## Presale is done, Discovery is next

We ran a successful presale.
We agreed on the idea with the client.
Next up: Discovery.

And there is work here for more than just the people who talk to the client.

```diagram
type: steps
title: From presale to the start of work
- Presale | The idea is agreed with the client
- Discovery | While we clarify details with the client, the team checks its own part in parallel
- Team findings | Go back into the discussion with the client
- Requirements, budget or the whole idea | Can change before anything starts
```

## What we find out on the client side

While we find out from the client:

- How will we measure the result?
- Who signs off on the final decision?
- Who are our users and [key stakeholders](/blog/when-the-client-wants-to-control-every-step/)?
- What are the budget, deadlines and hard constraints?
- Where will the business make money, save money or reduce risk?
- What problem do we need to solve, and what do we know about it right now?

## What the team can check before there are any tasks

The team has its own things to find out and check:

```diagram
type: cards
mark: check
title: What the team checks during Discovery
- Do we know how to build this?
- Which regulatory requirements apply?
- What information is missing, and where do we get it?
- Which systems and contractors do we depend on?
- Which ready-made solutions can we reuse?
- Which assumptions and hypotheses still need testing?
- Do we have enough people, skills, access and infrastructure?
- What can we already start on, and what needs clarifying and checking first?
```

## Bring the team's findings back to the client

The team's findings go back into the [discussion with the client](/blog/why-one-way-reporting-kills-projects/).
They can change the requirements, the budget or the whole idea.

Nothing has started yet, and look how much work there already is!!!
