The Descartes square is a cold shower for management fantasies.

## The four questions of the Descartes square

It's a simple tool made of four questions:

```diagram
type: matrix
title: The Descartes square
x: we do it / we don't do it
y: will happen / won't happen
- What will happen if we do it?
- What will happen if we don't?
- What won't happen if we do it?
- What won't happen if we don't?
```

## Answers in money, time, risk and responsibility only

In management it only works when the answers are tied to money, time, risk and [responsibility](/blog/project-manager-seniority-is-responsibility/).
Fantasies, dreams and assumptions have no place here.

```visual
type: hub
title: What every answer is measured in
center: The square's answer
- Money | money
- Time | clock
- Risk | warning
- Responsibility | shield
```

## When the Descartes square fits and when it is overkill

You don't need it for every question.

```visual
type: versus
title: The Descartes square: worth it or overkill
left: Overkill
right: Worth it
lefticon: x
righticon: check
tone: neutral
- A routine decision: there is a protocol and an owner | An expensive, irreversible crisis decision
- The data is in, the decision is obvious | A doubt: priority or impulse
- A basic question that needs no extra tools | Emotions and pressure hide the cost of haste
```

## What the Descartes square gives you, and where it stops

It shows you the cost of acting, the result of doing nothing, the risks and the losses.

Just don't forget that it only looks at a binary choice: "do it / don't do it". And there are alternatives:

```diagram
type: cards
mark: arrow
title: Beyond "do it / don't do it"
- Do part of it
- Test it on a small segment
- Find another way
```

## A square without a decision

The Descartes square gives you clarity and speed.

A square without a decision strokes your ego while the system just stands still.
