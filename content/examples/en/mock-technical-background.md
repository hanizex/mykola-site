### 1. Soft skills

It is clear that they are at the highest level, even in small details. You have well-developed communication skills, you know how to lead a conversation, you express your thoughts quite clearly, and you understand the needs of other people. This is very important for a manager, and I am glad that you have no problems here.

### 2. Hard skills

You have learned everything about working in a product company in your field quite well. You know the processes, the requirements and the details, even at the technical level, thanks to your experience as a developer and your active part in solving technical questions. On the other hand, your management experience comes from only one company in one field, so you lack experience in some areas. On one side, I can see that you prepare yourself in theory and connect theory to practice. On the other side, when a question is about a field you do not know, your confidence drops a lot, and this can affect the interview. To raise your confidence, I advise you to read the main theoretical questions about project management and prepare your answers to them: [https://dou.ua/lenta/articles/interview-questions-project-manager/](https://dou.ua/lenta/articles/interview-questions-project-manager/)

### 3. Clarity of answers

You are very smart, so you can build a quite correct answer based on your experience and observations. But this answer will be in your own words and in your own terminology. If the interviewer cares about generally accepted standards of definitions and wording, they may not like your answers, even if the answers are correct in substance. I would advise you to check which words trusted sources use when they write about different aspects of project management, and learn to express your thoughts briefly, in formal language. After that, you can add your own view and practical experience to these answers, if that is expected from you. I think you will do this easily.

### 4. Body language, gestures, non-verbal vocabulary and tone of voice

When we discuss processes and events you know, you radiate confidence and expertise in every way I can see. As soon as the discussion moves to topics that are unfamiliar to you, or that you think are difficult, everything flips: from an expert you turn into an unsure beginner. I would like to tell you that even in the areas where you feel unsure and vulnerable, your intuition works quite well, and you have no reason to be so worried and nervous. I hope my feedback will help you be more confident at your next interviews. I give you official permission to feel confident and relaxed. You will not say something stupid if you think aloud about a topic that is not in your practical experience. The reason is simple: you have a quite good management mindset, and you can rely on it.

### 5. Management tools

We did not raise this topic during the interview, and that is my fail. But I noticed that during our conversation you did not mention a single tool that you have used. Jira, Confluence, Slack, Figma, Miro, Draw io, Google Sheets, Excel, Postman etc. Your CV does not list tools either. I advise you not to forget to say how and with what you do your work. This is also important. "When we work on risks, we make a list of the main known and most probable ones. I put it all in a Google Sheets table and attach it in Confluence, so it is easy to access" - and that is already 2 mentions. You do not need to describe all the tools in every answer, especially when they repeat, but it is useful to mention the main ones.

### 6. Interest in the company/job

I cannot judge this from a test interview, because I am not a potential employer, and whether you get an offer did not depend on me. But I hope that at real interviews you show interest and motivation. When I offered you a chance to get a recommendation from me to my company, I did not see any enthusiasm. And after I mentioned a condition that does not suit you, I immediately saw a "valid refusal". I do not know your situation, how your finances are, or whether you really need to look for a job. But I get the impression that you have no urgent need (yet), and you look for a job simply because it would be good to have a job. Do you feel the difference? I may be wrong in my judgments and observations, I admit that. But if I am right, it will be harder for you to get an offer, exactly because the company still has to "deserve" you as an employee. The market now does not work in favour of job seekers. Few people will agree to please candidates and persuade them. Think about your motivation and goals. And talk about them during interviews. People must see and understand that you really aim for long-term, fruitful cooperation, and that you did not just come to practise interviews.

### 7. Way of searching

You respond to vacancies on job sites. These are so-called "cold applications". I advise you to switch to working "hot": from time to time, post on LinkedIn that you are looking for a job. Write about what kind of job you are looking for and what you expect, and also about yourself: what you can do, what problems you solve, what achievements you have, and so on (it does not all have to be in one post, you can write a series of short posts, each covering one side). I also advise you to talk more with recruiters from different companies and to give them this task directly: "help me find a job"... strangely, many of them will do nothing until you stop only asking and say it in exactly this commanding form. Contacting recruiting agencies can also be quite effective.
