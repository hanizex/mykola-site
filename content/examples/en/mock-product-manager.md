### 1. Theoretical knowledge

This is definitely not worked out enough. I can see that you learn product management from your practical experience in companies. You know some terminology, but you get lost in definitions and areas of responsibility, because they are blurry for you. As I said, I advise you to raise your level of theoretical preparation. This immediately moves you one level up in grade.

### 2. Tool skills

Here everything is fine. You know the main tools and can use them effectively. You also understand what data you need for analysis, and you can clearly define what information you want to get as a result. This shows that your technical skills are fine.

### 3. Intuitive management and adaptability

You have no theory and no classical education, so you adapt intuitively. And you do it quite well. I would say that your ability to adapt is really impressive. You quickly learn everything you take on, and this is your strong card. I advise you to present it nicely in your CV (not just "I learn new things fast", but "to improve the KPI of my work, I learned the technique XXX in ZZZ time", or something in this style

### 4. Integration into the product and the team

"We did the analytics", "We proposed", "We implemented". You think in terms of the whole team and you see yourself as one whole with it. This is quite cool when it comes to being deeply involved in the processes. On the other hand, it blurs your own achievements and takes away responsibility for your personal actions. It can stop you from fairly judging your own influence and contribution to the work.

### 5. Main motivation

Money is great, but when it is your only incentive and motivator, it can become a reason not to make you an offer. There is a very high chance that you would easily leave your current job just because another place offers more money. And even the previous point about "integration with the team" may not hold you. I advise you to think about what else motivates you and keeps you in a company when hard times come (and they come quite regularly anywhere)

### 6. Lack of ambition and vision of your future

This point is somewhat connected to the previous one. When I asked about your personal development plan, you answered that you are going to do a "personal retrospective" and, based on it, decide where to move next. There is a risk that you will want to move in a direction other than product management. This makes people wonder whether a company should hire a person who has not decided what they want. I recommend that you define 1-2 goals using OKR or SMART and follow them. Or at least you can say them out loud during interviews, and this will add to your chances of getting an offer

### 7. Calm and confidence

I liked that during the interview you looked confident and behaved openly. Of course, your future did not depend on this interview, so that could play a role. I hope you behave the same way at real interviews. It is also very good that you have specific questions about the companies where you interview. It shows your interest, which is also important and works in your favour.

### 8. Clarity of answers

There are some difficulties here. Several times you answered not the question I asked, but the one you heard. I had to bring you back and insist on the answer I expected to hear. Recruiters and managers may not do this. They may simply conclude that you are not very attentive, and that this can affect the quality of your work. I advise you to pay more attention to how the question is asked and what exactly is being asked. You can even ask again whether you understood the question correctly. Such clarifications will work in your favour and add weight to your professionalism.

### 9. Amount of "water" in answers

There were several situations when you gave too much context in your answers, or even flew off and started to talk about something unrelated. I had to stop you and return you to a constructive conversation. This shows you as an open and sincere person. But on the other hand, it smudges your communication skills a bit, and it can give the impression that you easily lose attention and cannot focus on a goal. On the other hand, for some people sincerity and honesty can be more important than being collected and focused. So here I can only advise you to pay more attention to the person you are talking to and to their reaction to the "extra context"

In my opinion, you are quite a good product manager, and I would recommend you for the position of Strong Junior Product Manager, with a prospect of fast promotion to Middle
