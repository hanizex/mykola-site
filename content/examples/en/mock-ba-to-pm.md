### 1. Soft skills, self-presentation

Overall, your soft skills are quite well developed, and I can see that you work on them, mostly by getting feedback from other people. You put special focus on the skills you need for your actual work, and that is very good.

### 2. Hard skills, tools, processes

Thanks to your previous experience and strong intelligence, you learn the technical skills you need easily. You are not afraid of challenges when you need to try a new tool or approach. I can feel your confidence in your own abilities and in the things you work with.

### 3. Body language, gestures, non-verbal signs and tone of voice

During the whole interview you controlled yourself quite well. You said you were nervous, but it did not stop you from staying professional. When we discussed unfamiliar topics, or when there were distractions, you behaved in a fully adequate and dignified way. I liked your ability to keep eye contact, even in remote communication through a webcam.

### 4. Motivation and interest

It is clear that you are a motivated person and that you are really interested in what you do. You willingly spend time on self-study and self-development, and you look for feedback to improve your weak areas and grow as a professional.

### 5. Self-confidence

Despite all your achievements, you are not very confident in yourself, and you undervalue your experience and abilities. You make compromises with yourself even before others give you any requirements. You are afraid that you will not cope, and this fear limits your career growth.

### 6. Theoretical knowledge

You need to work on theory. I advise you to go through the main points of the methodologies and frameworks again. Define clearly the list of direct duties and the area of responsibility of a product manager, and how they work with other stakeholders. Your practical experience is limited, especially in IT, so you cannot rely on it too much. But in general, your understanding of the basics is quite correct. You just need to structure it and put it in order.

### 7. Clear thinking and speech

Several times I had to stop you and ask for a more concrete and clear answer. Sometimes I had to repeat my question, because while you were thinking you moved quite far from the point of the question. I would advise you to google the main questions that product managers get at interviews and practise giving the clearest possible answers to them. The advice from the previous point about improving theoretical knowledge will help here too.

### 8. Management and leadership skills

You easily and confidently take responsibility for products, projects and people. You can motivate, teach and inspire. You know how to lead people, and you also understand well when you need to listen to other people's opinions, and you can judge whether they are reasonable and relevant. I would advise you to pay attention to things like delegating duties and sharing responsibility. You cannot carry everything on yourself. This is a dead end, and the resources of one person are not enough to do everything in every direction. Learning to delegate will open new paths for you and unlock many additional internal and external resources) I remind you that asking for help is normal, and sometimes it is simply the best way.
