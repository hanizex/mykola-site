### 1. Clean speech

There were a lot of "uhhh..." sounds. You fill every pause with this sound. It is not critical, but some people may see it as a sign that you are unsure about what you say, or even as a waste of time. Try to record yourself on video and check in which moments the "uhh" was not appropriate.

### 2. Clean answers

When you refer to your experience, you add a lot of unnecessary details, or you completely leave the topic of the question and start telling something unrelated. Sometimes you bring yourself back to the topic of the conversation in time, and sometimes you get carried away. In conversations with people this can sometimes be useful, but at interviews it more often shows that your attention is scattered. It is not very critical, but it is worth paying attention to.

### 3. "Straight-A student" complex

You do not admit that you do not know an answer, and you give your own version. Sometimes it is wrong, but you keep a misleadingly confident face and tone of voice. Other non-verbal signs give you away, but you need to know how to read them. This can lead to risks in setting tasks, defining goals and checking results. The risk is high. Pay attention to this. Try to listen to yourself and notice when you are sure about what is being discussed, and when you are just "wriggling out of the situation".

### 4. Control of meaning

I did not hear a single clarifying question, even when I asked a question on purpose in an imprecise and vague way. Instead, I got a flow of options and hypotheses. They did not sound like clarifications, they blurred the topic even more. I had to stop you and make my question more specific. Not everyone can do this, so there is a big risk of losing valuable information by losing it in countless variations of fantasies.

### 5. Theoretical preparation

In general, I would rate it as average (normal). But you said you got certificates and took courses, so... my expectations were a bit higher. You should improve the main points where you feel unsure.

### 6. Relying on your own experience

Everything you said was based only on your personal experience, which mostly comes from work in another field, in different roles. So there is a lot mixed in it, and it does not match the processes and practices that work in other companies. Your experience has some value, but it is important to also use the experience of other people. A good knowledge of methodologies, practices, approaches and ways of implementation will help, and you can get it from books about project management. You can also share experience you got from other managers. This will show people your ability to learn from other people's examples.

### 7. Generalisation

There were many generalisations in your answers. I missed concrete examples, especially when I asked for them directly. Instead of "I would find out their motivation", say "I would look at my notes about this developer to remember what motivates them best, or I would ask HR, or I would ask them directly".

And do not forget my advice to ask recruiters and agencies for help. All this feedback about the interview will not help if there are no interviews)
