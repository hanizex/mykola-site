### 1. Hard Skills

I would rate your hard skills as a good middle level. It is clear that you know some tools well, within the tasks you have had to do. There were not many of these situations, so your experience is limited. I also see that you try to use the tools and approaches you know in situations that are new to you. This is interesting, but a bit risky.

### 2. Soft Skills

Your soft skills are well developed. You are attentive and gentle, and you adjust instantly when the tone of the conversation changes. You tend to make communication with you as pleasant and easy as possible. My impression is that this stops you from pushing other people where it is needed to get a result, and overall it can work against you. I want to point out that being firm sometimes is not the same as being harsh most of the time. One helps you, the other gets in your way.

### 3. Theoretical Knowledge

You lack theoretical knowledge. You were lucky that the places where you worked followed fairly good management practices, and you learned useful things from experience. But to grow further you will definitely need theory, because it will be your solid ground for making decisions. This will be especially important in a delivery manager role, because the financial success of the areas you are responsible for will depend directly on your decisions.

### 4. Practical Experience

As I said above, your practical experience is quite good, but not complete. It will work well for similar companies and projects, but it is not enough for growth and development. There are many gaps in your knowledge, and because of them it can be hard to take on new projects (but you have to). I recommend joining a pet project from the very start, or launching your own. A good condition for getting this practice is that you must go through all the stages of the project life cycle that you have missed so far.

### 5. Body language, gestures, facial expressions, tone of voice

Your body was tense almost the whole meeting. Your voice was tense too. It looks like you were nervous and worried, and this showed in sharp movements, in the way your eyes moved around, in your posture, and so on. From time to time I managed to calm you down, but not for long. When I pushed you into even more stress, it was easy to do, but then it was hard to calm you again. How do you work with your anxiety? Do you work with it at all?

### 6. Confident Uncertainty

Too often you used two opposite words in one sentence, for example "of course" and "probably", or "I know" and "most likely". So you start with confidence in your words (and your body shows at once that you are bluffing), and then a few words later you admit that you do not really know the exact or correct answer. If it were not for your gentleness and charisma, this would be annoying and would make people lose patience. Still, I am not sure you can always rely on that. Sooner or later a moment will come when people will not forgive it. Because in essence this is a lie, even if it is well hidden and softened.

### 7. Ambition and Adventurism

Moving into a delivery role with your current understanding of the topic is a bold plan. On top of that, I did not see any doubt in your intentions. It looks like this will happen anyway, and the only question is whether sooner or later. I will not try to talk you out of it. My impression is that in the past you were often in situations where you went into the unknown and succeeded, at a level that was acceptable to you. This time you have a chance too. And the chance will grow if you improve your theory and practice. But even without that, there is a chance that you will learn on the go, you are good at that! I hope that if it does not work out the first time, you will not give up and will keep trying)
