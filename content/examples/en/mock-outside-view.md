### 1. Hard Skills

You know many tools. You understand the methods and approaches, you know the sequences, and you understand why each step matters. Some tools you try to use for a purpose they were not made for, but you are aware of it, and you do it because you do not have a more suitable tool at hand. In general, the Pareto principle works for project management tools, as it does in many other areas: with 20% of the tools you can solve 80% of the tasks. You are going in the right direction. Study different things, and improve the ones you are best at

### 2. Soft Skills

Developing your soft skills is what I advise you to focus on. In waves, I had the impression that you understand communication and interaction well, and then the opposite: that you are completely lost and rely only on instructions you learned by heart, or you go "from the problem", when you got burned somewhere and do not want to repeat that painful experience. Also, when I asked questions about the soft skills of other people, you did not go into this topic and moved to hard skills.

### 3. Theoretical Knowledge

Your theoretical knowledge is at a medium level. There are many gaps, but the knowledge you have is confirmed by practical experience or well thought through, and it becomes a strong support for making decisions. Preparing for certification should fill the gaps and make your theoretical base even stronger. Please, when you study theory, try to find similar situations in your previous experience. This way the information will stay in your memory better.

### 4. Practical Experience

You learned well, in your previous jobs, the processes that were set up there and the approaches that were used. This shows that you learn fast and well. If you had more theoretical knowledge, you could get more practical experience, because you would see more nuances to pay attention to. This is one more reason for you to keep updating your theory, and not only from PMBoK, but in general to look at what happens in practice and how.

### 5. Body language, gestures, facial expressions, tone of voice

You control yourself well in conversation, both when everything goes smoothly and when something unexpected happens. Your voice does not shake, and your tone does not jump. Your facial expressions and gestures show a person who is hard to knock off balance. You have a great set of "trump poses and movements", like an experienced actor, and you know how to use the right pose or gesture so that it looks quite natural.

### 6. Team Management

Here I cannot understand for sure: either you simply have little experience building and managing teams, or I asked the questions wrongly and you understood me differently, or you really see team members as chess pieces on a board. Each one has a function and a set of technical parameters (hard skills), and based on this you build your vision of the strategy and the possible result of working together. If you focus only on "technical parameters" and ignore people's personal qualities, this limits your options.

### 7. Learning and Development

These are your strongest qualities. You learn fast and in depth. What you have learned, you easily use in practice. It is clear that the most frequent trigger for learning was some difficult situation in life or at work. To have fewer of such hard events in the future, I recommend that you experiment more often at work. For example, from time to time ask yourself: "How else can this be done?", "What else can this mean?", "What else should I pay attention to?", "What do I know about this situation, and where do I know it from?".. There are many more open questions that help develop critical thinking, and critical thinking will show you possible directions for growth.
